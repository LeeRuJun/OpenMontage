# Story Writing Director

A story is a chain of consequential choices. Information becomes narrative only when a person, group, or institution wants something, meets resistance, acts, and causes a changed situation.

## Research-first workflow

Do not draft prose before the research material inventory and material density map are complete. Use this order:

1. **Research material inventory** — collect a source-backed event inventory: event cards, dates, places, actors, actions, consequences, uncertainty, and open questions.
2. **Material density map** — classify each item as `core_event`, `support`, `bridge`, or `commentary`; assign a target time/space and record what must not be repeated.
3. **Causal story spine** — choose the order in which choices and consequences change the situation.
4. **Scene contracts** — turn each core event into a starting state, objective, resistance, attempt, reversal, consequence, and next pressure.
5. **Prose** — write only after the previous records pass review.

A short draft is a research problem, not a license to add explanation. Add a verified event or reduce the promised duration.

## Story blueprint

Before prose, lock:

- **Premise**: the situation and the change the story promises;
- **Subject/protagonist**: whose choices carry the chain;
- **Desire**: what they want now, not a vague theme;
- **Opposition**: who or what blocks that desire; the opposition must create a concrete obstacle;
- **Stakes**: what is lost if the choice fails;
- **Knowledge state**: what each important actor knows, believes, hides, or has not yet learned;
- **Planted elements**: promises, objects, relationships, routes, or facts that must return or resolve;
- **Ending change**: what is materially different after the final event.

Keep fixed decisions separate from alternatives. Keep verified facts separate from interpretation, intention, and unresolved possibility.

## Scene contract

Every scene or narrated segment gets a contract before drafting. The immediate objective must be concrete, and the resistance must be observable in the scene:

1. **Starting state** — time, place, people, and the situation inherited from the previous event;
2. **Immediate objective** — what the active subject tries to obtain, prevent, decide, or reach;
3. **Resistance** — the person, rule, distance, uncertainty, or material obstacle that blocks it;
4. **Attempt** — the concrete action taken in the scene;
5. **Reversal/discovery** — what changes the available choices;
6. **Consequence** — the new situation created by the attempt;
7. **Continue question** — the unresolved pressure that makes the next scene necessary;
8. **Information boundary** — what is revealed, withheld, or still unknown.

A segment that only explains background has no scene contract. Attach the context to an action, arrival, order, confrontation, discovery, departure, loss, or decision.

## Causal outline

Build the event chain before polishing sentences:

```text
starting state → objective → resistance → action → reversal → consequence → next objective
```

Check every arrow. If event B would happen unchanged even if event A were removed, event A is not yet doing story work. Repair the motivating choice or the consequence; do not merely add commentary between them.

## Drafting and revision

- Draft scenes after the blueprint, not the other way around.
- Use concrete verbs and sensory place details; let the reader infer the theme from what changes.
- Keep analysis subordinate to action. One short interpretation may follow a fully narrated event; it may not replace one.
- Diagnose in layers: event/causality first, motivation and continuity second, pacing third, sentences last.
- After revision, update the event record, knowledge states, planted elements, and all dependent scenes.

## Topic adapters

A historical adapter adds evidence IDs, dates, source certainty, and a rule against invented dialogue. A brand adapter adds product facts and audience stakes. A fictional adapter adds world rules and invented continuity. The causal scene contract stays the same.

## Natural Chinese revision

Borrow the useful editing principle from Chinese long-form writing workflows without importing their fictional content:

- Replace emotion explanation with **observable behavior**, concrete objects, spoken choices, and consequences.
- Reduce stock gestures, vague modifiers, repeated negations, excessive punctuation, and formulaic contrast (`不是……而是……`) when they do not carry a real reversal.
- Keep separate records for underlying reality, each character's knowledge, and what the audience has already been told.
- Diagnose the reading problem first and **revise in context**; preserve established events and hidden information while changing only the dependent passage.
- `不要为了字数`添加无关事件、评论或重复环境描写；如果时长不足，补查并加入真正改变选择空间的 verified event.
- A detector or clean scan is only an editing signal, never a substitute for narrative judgment.
